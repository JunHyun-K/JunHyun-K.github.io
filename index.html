I am interested in optimization theory for machine learning: stochastic min–max optimization, adaptive optimization, and the geometry of representations. I am interested in the limits of what optimization methods can guarantee, and in how they actually behave. Research

Min–max problems and variational inequalities

Variational inequalities include min–max problems and the equilibrium conditions of games. I study stochastic methods for them when the noise variance can grow with the iterate [1] [2], and guarantees for the last iterate that hold at every iteration, without a horizon fixed in advance [2].

Stepsize using stochastic curvature information

A line search adapts the stepsize to local curvature, but a step accepted on one sample need not decrease the full objective. I study when stepsizes chosen from samples still come with guarantees: a line search with the STORM estimator, without interpolation [3], and adaptive gradient descent with increasing batches [4]. In both, the stepsize follows local smoothness instead of one global constant.

Geometry of learned representations

When embeddings are trained with cross-entropy to predict neighbouring nodes in a graph, their geometry can reflect the shape of the graph. I study how this geometry depends on the loss and the model, using KKT conditions and eigenvalue spectra.
